How to read these numbers

With perfect fills at the breakout price, the same rules made 45% a year (worst fall 21%, ₹10 lakh to ₹30.5 lakh in three years). Plan against the realistic figure above.

The test uses today's index members, so companies that later collapsed or delisted are missing, which flatters every number. Simply owning all of those same stocks returned 33% a year over the same period. The edge to judge is when to buy and how much to risk, and the live test below decides whether it holds.

Past results do not promise future returns.

Ideas that were tested and thrown out
Buying only on good fundamentalsIn this market price leads the reported numbers. Every fundamental filter on entries made results worse, so fundamentals rank and veto, they never trigger a buy.
Buying only in hot sectorsRestricting buys to the hottest sectors cut the average result per trade by more than 80%. Sector heat stays as context.
Holding more positions at onceGoing from 12 to 20 positions lowered the result per trade by about a third: the extra slots only buy weaker breakouts.
Risking more after a winning streakScaling risk with the portfolio's own recent results cost 4 to 6 points a year. The market-breadth rule already does that job.
Buying on good newsStocks with positive filings went on to break out less often than average (7% against 17%). News stays a risk check, never a buy signal.
Trading biggerRaising the risk per trade from 1.25% to 1.75% or 2.5% broke the drawdown limit twice. Size stayed where the evidence put it.
