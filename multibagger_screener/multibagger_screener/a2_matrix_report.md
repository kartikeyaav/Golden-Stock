## Post-run disclosure (appended 2026-07-31, not generated by the script)

**The MAR bar of 3.58 was mis-specified**, identically to A1: it is the
breakout+EP figure, while this harness is breakout-only and its control
reproduces at **MAR 2.69**. The bar was not lowered after the fact. A2_a **also
fails the like-for-like reading (2.55 vs 2.69)**, so the rejection does not
depend on the error.

**Four of five registered checks passed.** The class is real and profitable:
32 entries at **1.106R**, CAGR **+2.9pp**, and — unlike the A1 sleeve — it
**improves the chop cohort** (P2 0.342 -> 0.463), which is the system's known
regime weakness. The binding constraint is drawdown: **-19.74% vs -17.64%**,
which drags risk-adjusted return below the control.

**Parameter sensitivity is the real finding.** The IPO class returns
**0.502R / 1.106R / 2.436R** across three reasonable settings on samples of
21-32 trades. With a ~9:1 payoff at n≈30, a handful of trades carry everything.
A2_b's 2.436R is NOT evidence that the 90-bar variant works; the spread IS the
noise floor.

**Untested and deliberately not proposed as an adoption path:** a half-risk IPO
sleeve with its own slots (the A1_a shape). Drawdown is precisely what halving
risk targets — which is why proposing it now, after seeing that DD is what
failed, would be post-hoc criterion selection. Any such test starts as a fresh
pre-registration committed before it runs, and inherits the noise caution above.

**Disposition: MEASURED, NOT ADOPTED. Zero capital.** `generate_ipo_signals`
stays in the engine, wired into no live path, so this is repeatable as the
sample grows.
